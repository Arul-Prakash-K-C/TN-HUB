Key Findings
1. Contradictory sign-in state     HIGH
Observed: A returning user with saved credentials is shown a banner urging them to register as a 'New User' immediately after the page loads, despite an existing account being detected.
Impact: Creates doubt about whether an existing account is still valid, leading some users to attempt duplicate registration.
Fix direction: Show one unambiguous authentication state at a time. Never surface a 'register' prompt to a session that already has valid stored credentials.
2. Non-functional 'apply' links     HIGH  
Observed: In the master services table, the 'link to apply' column is identical for every one of the 100+ services and simply points back to the homepage rather than the specific service.
Impact: A user who finds their service in the table gains no shortcut — they must restart navigation from scratch, effectively doubling the effort of using the table at all.
Fix direction: Every service listing must deep-link directly to its own application entry point.
3. Unfiltered, undifferentiated service catalog     HIGH  
Observed: All services across all departments (Fisheries, Traffic Police, Revenue, Civil Supplies, Anna University, and more) are listed in one continuous table with no search, filter, or categorization by common citizen need.
Impact: Finding a specific, commonly needed service (e.g. a Community Certificate) requires scanning a long undifferentiated list rather than a guided path.
Fix direction: Group services by everyday life-events (certificates, ration card, fines, admissions) with search-as-you-type, and surface the most-requested services first.
4. Missing or inaccurate document requirements     HIGH  
Observed: Several services list their required documents simply as 'NA', even for applications that clearly require supporting documents (e.g. a new ration card application).
Impact: Citizens cannot prepare in advance and may travel to a service center or attempt an online submission without the correct paperwork, causing rejected or delayed applications.
Fix direction: Every service must carry an accurate, maintained document checklist shown before the application starts, not buried in a separate FAQ page.
5. No processing-time expectations     MEDIUM  
Observed: The 'Stipulated Timeline' column is blank for every listed service.
Impact: Citizens have no way to know whether a delay is normal or a sign something has gone wrong, increasing anxiety and repeat helpdesk contact.
Fix direction: Publish a realistic turnaround range per service, and reflect it again on the status-tracking screen.
6. No native grievance or complaint system     MEDIUM  
Observed: The portal's own 'Grievances/Complaints' page contains no form — only two outbound links to separate government sites (a CM helpline portal and a CM cell site).
Impact: A citizen with an issue on this portal must leave it entirely and re-explain their problem on an unrelated system, with no connection back to their original application.
Fix direction: Build a grievance form scoped to a specific application/reference number, submitted and tracked inside the same product.
7. Fragmented status-tracking across multiple URLs     MEDIUM  
Observed: Status checks are documented across at least three separate endpoints depending on which flow a citizen originally used, with no single canonical page.
Impact: Users are often unsure which status page applies to their application, and may check the wrong one and conclude their application is missing.
Fix direction: One unified status page, keyed to the application reference number, with a plain-language explanation of each stage.
8. Superficial accessibility support     MEDIUM  
Observed: The only accessibility accommodation present is a font-size toggle (A-/A/A+). There is no Tamil-first language default, no mobile-optimized layout for the dense data tables, and no simplified-language mode.
Impact: Excludes exactly the users the portal most needs to serve well: rural residents, older citizens, and first-time digital users on mobile devices.
Fix direction: Mobile-first responsive layout, a genuine Tamil/English toggle, larger touch targets, and plain-language copy throughout.
9. No post-submission confirmation or next steps     LOW  
Observed: Publicly available flow documentation shows no clear confirmation screen describing what happens immediately after an application is submitted.
Impact: Citizens are left uncertain whether their submission succeeded and what to expect next.
Fix direction: A clear confirmation screen with reference number, expected timeline, and how to check status or file a follow-up.
Findings from the Logged-In Citizen Dashboard
The following issues were observed inside the authenticated account area, using the account holder's own session. They point to deeper reliability problems beyond the public-facing pages already covered above.
10. Service tabs don't reliably render their own content     HIGH  
Observed: The Services screen offers four tabs — Department Wise, Service Wise, Search, and Public. Clicking 'Service Wise' or 'Search' visibly highlights the tab as selected, but the panel underneath frequently keeps showing the previous tab's content instead of switching.
Impact: Users cannot trust that what's on screen reflects what they clicked. Someone trying to search may believe search is broken or unavailable, when the underlying feature does exist.
Fix direction: Tab selection state and rendered content must be tightly coupled and tested; a tab should never appear active while showing stale content.
11. Tab labels don't match their actual content     HIGH  
Observed: The keyword search box (a 'Keyword' field with a search icon) appeared under the tab labeled 'Public', not under the tab labeled 'Search'. Clicking 'Search' instead displayed a leftover results table from a prior action.
Impact: This is not just a state bug but a labeling mismatch — a citizen looking for the search feature is actively misdirected to the wrong tab by its name.
Fix direction: Audit every tab's label against its actual rendered content; the tab named 'Search' must contain the search input, consistently.
12. 500+ services with no default search or filter     HIGH  
Observed: The Service Wise view (when it does render) confirms the catalog holds 524 individual services, paginated 10 at a time, sorted alphabetically by service code rather than by common citizen need. The default landing tab, Department Wise, offers no search at all.
Impact: A citizen has to already know which of 500+ codes and departments their need falls under before they can even begin narrowing down, since the most useful tool (search) is not the default and is itself unreliable (see finding 10–11).
Fix direction: Make search-by-need the default landing experience, not a secondary tab a user has to discover.
13. Inconsistent empty states across pages     MEDIUM  
Observed: 'Pending Transactions' shows a clear message: 'INFO: No Pending Transactions Found.' The 'Transaction Details' page, by contrast, loads its heading and then renders a completely blank body with no message at all.
Impact: A blank screen with no explanation reads as a broken page rather than an empty state, and offers no next step.
Fix direction: Standardize a single empty-state pattern across every list/table view, always with a short explanation and a clear next action.
14. No orientation on the post-login dashboard     MEDIUM  
Observed: Immediately after login, the Dashboard shows only a month picker and a 'Service Wise Transactions — No Transactions Found' panel with a 'Total Transactions: 0' counter. There is no welcome context, no shortcut to frequently used services, and no summary of account status.
Impact: New and returning users alike land on a page that offers nothing actionable, especially unhelpful for a first-time applicant who has never made a transaction yet.
Fix direction: Replace the bare zero-state with clear starting points: 'Apply for a new service', 'Track an existing application', or recently viewed services.
15. Feedback form drops the user out of their logged-in session context     MEDIUM  
Observed: Clicking 'Feedback' in the account sidebar navigates to a page that no longer shows the sidebar or the logged-in account name — it renders like a public, logged-out page — and asks the user to manually re-enter Name, Mobile Number, and Email even though they are authenticated.
Impact: Breaks the sense of a continuous authenticated session and creates redundant data entry for information the system already has.
Fix direction: Feedback submitted from within an authenticated session should pre-fill known account details and preserve the logged-in layout.
16. Citizen Charter is a stale, unmaintained document     MEDIUM  
Observed: The 'Citizen Charter' sidebar link opens a static PDF file named 'rev_e_cc_2015.pdf' — a Revenue Department charter dated 2015, over a decade old, with no indication of whether it has been reviewed since.
Impact: Citizens relying on this document for service standards or timelines may be reading commitments that are no longer accurate.
Fix direction: Publish charters as maintained, dated pages (not static legacy PDFs), with a visible 'last reviewed' date.
